🩺 My Personalized Care Plan

Complex medical terminology translated into straightforward, everyday steps you and your family can follow at home.

In plain terms: Diabetes means your body has elevated sugar levels in the bloodstream. Over time, high blood sugar can weaken the nerves and blood circulation in your feet, making it harder to feel minor cuts or pressure. A sore formed on your right foot and developed into an ulcer, which required medical attention to ensure it heals properly without spreading.

✓
Why it matters:

Foot ulcers heal more slowly when blood sugar is high. Controlling both sugar levels and keeping pressure off the foot are equally important for healing.

Pressure is the biggest enemy of foot ulcer healing. When you stand or walk, body weight presses directly on the delicate granulating tissue.

🛑
No Prolonged Standing or Walking (2 Weeks):

Stay off your feet as much as possible for the first 14 days. Limit movement to essential trips (e.g., bathroom).

🛋️
Elevate Right Foot:

Whenever seated or lying down, prop your right leg up on 1-2 soft pillows so your foot is level with or slightly above your heart.

🚗
No Driving for 1 Week:

Do not drive any car, motorcycle, or scooter. Braking or pressing the accelerator pedal puts intense friction on the wound.

"Activity Guidance: Avoid prolonged standing/walking 2 weeks, elevate right foot, no pressure on wound, light indoor activities, no driving 1 week."

Follow this sterile protocol once daily (or if the dressing gets damp or soiled):

1
Wash Hands:

Wash hands with antiseptic soap for 20 seconds. Use clean disposable gloves if a family member is helping you.

2
Saline Wash:

Gently remove old dressing. Flush the wound using sterile Normal Saline (0.9% NaCl). Do NOT scrub. Pat dry around the edges with sterile gauze.

3
Apply Prescribed Ointments:

Apply Betadine (Povidone-Iodine) around the outer perimeter, and apply a thin layer of Mupirocin ointment directly over the ulcer bed.

4
Cover & Keep Dry:

Place sterile non-stick gauze pad over the wound and secure loosely with medical tape. Keep the bandage completely dry.

5
Never Walk Barefoot:

Never walk barefoot—even on indoor rugs or bathroom tiles. Always wear soft, seamless diabetic footwear.

💡 General First-Aid & Emergency Action Guidance General Clinical Suggestion
• If Blood Sugar drops below 70 mg/dL (Hypoglycemia): Immediately consume 15 grams of fast-acting sugar (e.g., 3 teaspoons of sugar dissolved in water, or 1/2 cup fruit juice). Rest for 15 minutes and re-check blood sugar. Repeat if still below 70 mg/dL. Notify doctor.
• If Dressing falls off or gets soaked: Do not panic. Clean your hands, gently rinse with sterile saline, re-apply prescribed ointment, cover with clean sterile gauze, and keep your foot elevated.
